# Organizational Diagnostic

### A structured framework for understanding what is actually limiting organizational performance.

When performance falls below expectations, the visible problem is rarely the whole problem.

Slower growth may be a commercial issue.

Or it may originate in strategy, leadership, organizational design, capability, economics, execution, or poor information.

Before changing the organization, diagnose the system.

> **Never redesign what you don't fully understand.**

---

## Purpose

The Organizational Diagnostic is designed to help leaders answer three questions:

1. Where is performance breaking down?
2. What is causing it?
3. What should leadership address first?

The objective is not to identify every problem.

The objective is to identify the **few constraints that materially affect organizational performance**.

---

# The 8 Dimensions

## 01. Strategy & Direction

### Core Question

**Does the organization know where it is going, where it will compete, and what it will prioritize?**

### Assess

- Strategic clarity
- Market attractiveness
- Competitive positioning
- Customer definition
- Value proposition
- Strategic priorities
- Resource allocation
- Alignment across leadership

### Executive Questions

- What are the three most important strategic priorities?
- Where will growth come from?
- Which customers and markets matter most?
- What are we deliberately choosing not to pursue?
- Are resources aligned with those choices?
- Would the leadership team give the same answers?

### Warning Signs

- Everything is a priority
- Strategy changes frequently
- Teams interpret priorities differently
- Resources are spread across too many initiatives
- Growth depends on activity rather than strategic choices

---

## 02. Financial Performance & Economics

### Core Question

**Does the business model create sustainable economic value?**

### Assess

- Revenue growth
- Gross margin
- EBITDA or operating contribution
- Unit economics
- Cost structure
- Cash generation
- Productivity
- Forecast accuracy
- Capital allocation

### Executive Questions

- Which products, markets, customers, and channels create the most economic value?
- Where are margins improving or deteriorating?
- What are the largest structural costs?
- Which activities consume resources without sufficient return?
- How reliable is the forecast?
- What must be true for the business to reach its financial objectives?

### Warning Signs

- Revenue grows while profitability deteriorates
- Leadership cannot explain unit economics
- Forecasts repeatedly miss
- Cost increases exceed productivity gains
- Resources remain committed to low-return activities

---

## 03. Commercial Engine

### Core Question

**Can the organization generate predictable, profitable, and scalable growth?**

### Assess

- Market segmentation
- Ideal customer profile
- Demand generation
- Sales productivity
- Pipeline health
- Conversion
- Pricing
- Partnerships
- Customer retention
- Revenue operations
- Forecasting

### Executive Questions

- Where does profitable growth actually come from?
- Which segments convert best?
- Is pipeline coverage sufficient?
- Where does conversion break down?
- How productive is each commercial channel?
- Are sales, marketing, partnerships, and customer success operating as one revenue system?

### Warning Signs

- Growth depends on individual performers
- Pipeline volume masks poor quality
- Sales and marketing use different definitions of success
- Forecast accuracy is low
- Customer acquisition costs rise without corresponding lifetime value
- Commercial activity increases without proportional revenue growth

---

## 04. Operating Model

### Core Question

**Is the organization designed to execute the strategy?**

### Assess

- Organizational structure
- Roles and accountability
- Decision rights
- Functional interfaces
- Processes
- Resource allocation
- Span of control
- Information flow
- Governance

### Executive Questions

- Is accountability clear?
- Who owns each critical business outcome?
- Where do decisions become slow?
- Which functions depend excessively on one another?
- Where is work duplicated?
- Does the structure support the strategy or reflect organizational history?

### Warning Signs

- Multiple people believe they own the same outcome
- Decisions escalate unnecessarily
- Functions optimize locally rather than organizationally
- Leadership becomes involved in routine operational decisions
- Processes depend on individual knowledge
- Organizational complexity grows faster than the business

---

## 05. Leadership

### Core Question

**Does the leadership system create clarity, accountability, and organizational capability?**

### Assess

- Leadership quality
- Accountability
- Decision-making
- Management capability
- Communication
- Coaching
- Performance management
- Succession
- Leadership alignment

### Executive Questions

- Does every leader understand what they own?
- Are difficult decisions made quickly enough?
- Do leaders solve problems or escalate them?
- Are managers developing stronger managers underneath them?
- Is poor performance addressed?
- Can the organization operate effectively without constant intervention from the CEO or MD?

### Warning Signs

- Accountability is ambiguous
- Poor performance persists
- Decisions repeatedly move upward
- Leaders protect functions rather than enterprise outcomes
- Management meetings focus on reporting instead of decisions
- The organization depends excessively on a few individuals

---

## 06. People & Capability

### Core Question

**Does the organization have the capabilities required to execute its strategy?**

### Assess

- Critical capabilities
- Talent quality
- Hiring
- Onboarding
- Productivity
- Skills gaps
- Performance distribution
- Retention
- Incentives
- Succession

### Executive Questions

- Which capabilities will determine future performance?
- Where are the largest capability gaps?
- Are the strongest people working on the most important problems?
- Do incentives reinforce the right behaviors?
- Can we attract and retain the talent required for the next stage?
- Which roles or capabilities will become constraints as we scale?

### Warning Signs

- Hiring substitutes for fixing productivity
- High performers compensate for weak systems
- Roles grow without clear accountability
- Capability gaps remain unresolved
- Incentives reward activity rather than outcomes
- Critical knowledge sits with individual employees

---

## 07. Execution & Management Cadence

### Core Question

**Can the organization consistently turn priorities into results?**

### Assess

- Goal setting
- KPIs
- Ownership
- Operating cadence
- Management meetings
- Cross-functional execution
- Escalation
- Follow-through
- Performance visibility

### Executive Questions

- Can every major priority be connected to an owner and measurable outcome?
- What gets reviewed weekly?
- What gets reviewed monthly?
- Are meetings producing decisions and actions?
- How quickly are constraints escalated?
- Are corrective actions tracked to completion?

### Warning Signs

- The same problems appear repeatedly
- Meetings produce discussion but few decisions
- KPIs exist without clear owners
- Teams confuse activity with progress
- Leadership discovers problems too late
- Strategic initiatives lose momentum after launch

---

## 08. Data, Technology & AI

### Core Question

**Does the organization have the information and technology required to make better decisions and execute efficiently?**

### Assess

- Data quality
- Management information
- Systems architecture
- Automation
- CRM and operational systems
- AI adoption
- Workflow design
- Technology ROI
- Decision support

### Executive Questions

- Do leaders trust the data?
- Can management see performance quickly enough to act?
- Where is manual work creating unnecessary cost or delay?
- Which workflows should be automated?
- Where can AI materially improve productivity, decisions, or customer experience?
- Are technology investments solving business problems or simply adding tools?

### Warning Signs

- Teams maintain competing versions of the truth
- Reporting requires extensive manual work
- Technology adoption is low
- Systems do not communicate effectively
- AI initiatives exist without measurable business outcomes
- Technology complexity increases faster than productivity

# Executive Scorecard

Score each dimension from **1 to 5**.

| Score | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **1** | Critical. Materially constraining performance. |
| **2** | Weak. Significant intervention required. |
| **3** | Functional. Adequate, but limiting future scale. |
| **4** | Strong. Supports current strategy and performance. |
| **5** | High-performing. Creates measurable competitive advantage. |

Then score the organization:

| Dimension | Score | Evidence | Priority |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Strategy & Direction | /5 | | |
| Financial Performance & Economics | /5 | | |
| Commercial Engine | /5 | | |
| Operating Model | /5 | | |
| Leadership | /5 | | |
| People & Capability | /5 | | |
| Execution & Management Cadence | /5 | | |
| Data, Technology & AI | /5 | | |

# From Diagnosis to Action

Do not treat every low score equally.

For each material constraint, determine:

### 1. Impact

How materially does this issue affect growth, profitability, customers, people, or execution?

### 2. Root Cause

What is creating the problem?

### 3. Urgency

What happens if nothing changes in the next 90 days?

### 4. Ownership

Which executive owns the outcome?

### 5. Intervention

What specifically needs to change?

---

## Prioritization

Classify interventions using two variables:

**Business Impact × Urgency**

Focus leadership attention on the small number of issues where both are high.

The diagnostic is complete only when it produces:

- A clear view of organizational health
- The critical constraints
- Evidence supporting the diagnosis
- Executive ownership
- A prioritized intervention plan
- Measurable outcomes
- A review cadence

---

# The Principle

> **Do not start with solutions.**
>
> **Understand the system. Identify the constraint. Fix what matters most.**
