# Executive Diagnostic Scorecard

### From organizational assessment to executive priorities.

The purpose of this scorecard is not to produce a perfect organizational rating.

It is to force leadership to make explicit judgments about performance, support those judgments with evidence, and identify the few constraints that require executive attention.

> **Score the evidence, not the narrative.**

---

## How to Use the Scorecard

Assess the organization across eight dimensions:

1. Strategy & Direction
2. Financial Performance & Economics
3. Commercial Engine
4. Operating Model
5. Leadership
6. People & Capability
7. Execution & Management Cadence
8. Data, Technology & AI

For each dimension:

1. Assign a score from 1 to 5.
2. Document the evidence behind the score.
3. Identify the principal constraint.
4. Determine the business impact.
5. Assign executive ownership.
6. Define the first intervention.

Do not score based on perception alone.

Use financial performance, operating data, customer evidence, employee information, management observation, and leadership interviews wherever possible.

---

# Scoring Standard

| Score | Status | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Critical | Materially damaging performance. Immediate executive intervention required. |
| **2** | Weak | Significant structural or execution gaps. Performance is being constrained. |
| **3** | Functional | Works today, but will constrain future performance or scale. |
| **4** | Strong | Consistently supports strategy and business performance. |
| **5** | High-Performing | Creates measurable organizational or competitive advantage. |

A score of **3 is not failure**.

It means the capability works, but leadership should determine whether it is sufficient for the organization's next stage.

# Prioritization Matrix

Evaluate each potential intervention against four criteria.

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| **Impact** | How materially will solving this improve business performance? |
| **Urgency** | What happens if leadership does nothing for 90 days? |
| **Controllability** | Can management materially influence the outcome? |
| **Dependency** | Does solving this unlock other improvements? |

Prioritize interventions with high impact, high urgency, meaningful management control, and strong downstream effects.

Each priority should have:

- One accountable executive
- A measurable outcome
- A defined KPI
- A review cadence
- A clear escalation path

Avoid creating a transformation plan with fifteen priorities.

**Three critical priorities executed well will usually create more value than fifteen initiatives executed partially.**

---

# Review Cadence

### Weekly

Review execution against the three priorities.

Focus on:

- Progress
- KPIs
- Constraints
- Decisions required
- Ownership
- Next actions

### Monthly

Reassess business impact and resource allocation.

Ask:

**Are we solving the right problems?**

### Quarterly

Repeat the organizational diagnostic.

Compare:

**Previous State → Intervention → Current State → Remaining Constraint**

The purpose is not to improve the score.

The purpose is to improve the organization.

---

# Executive Output

A completed diagnostic should allow leadership to state clearly:

**Where we are.**

**What is limiting performance.**

**What matters most.**

**Who owns it.**

**What will change in the next 90 days.**

**How we will know whether it worked.**
