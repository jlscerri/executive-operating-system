# 90-Day Executive Plan

### A practical system for turning an executive mandate into measurable business progress.

The first 90 days should not be treated as an extended orientation period.

An executive must understand the business quickly, establish the facts, identify the critical constraints, make decisions, create accountability, and begin improving performance.

The objective is not activity.

The objective is to establish control of the mandate and create measurable progress.

> Understand before redesigning. Act when the evidence is sufficient.

---

# The 90-Day Operating Logic

The plan follows four overlapping phases:

**Understand → Diagnose & Prioritize → Intervene → Prove & Institutionalize**

| Phase | Timing | Primary Objective | Core Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Understand | Day 0 to 15 | Establish facts and context | Business baseline |
| Diagnose & Prioritize | Day 16 to 30 | Identify material constraints | Executive priorities |
| Intervene | Day 31 to 60 | Change the drivers of performance | Active interventions |
| Prove & Institutionalize | Day 61 to 90 | Demonstrate progress and embed operating discipline | Measurable outcomes |

These phases are not rigid.

If a material problem is clear on Day 5, do not wait until Day 31 to act.

If the evidence is incomplete, do not redesign the organization simply to demonstrate activity.

# 01. Clarify the Mandate

Before changing the business, clarify what leadership expects.

The executive should establish:

- Why the mandate exists
- What problem must be solved
- What outcomes matter most
- What authority comes with the role
- What constraints already exist
- What cannot be compromised
- What the CEO or Board expects by Day 90
- What success should look like after 12 months

Use a simple mandate statement:

> My mandate is to ______ by ______ while protecting ______.

Example:

> My mandate is to restore profitable growth by rebuilding commercial execution and operating discipline while protecting customer retention and delivery quality.

If the mandate cannot be stated clearly, alignment is incomplete.

# 03. Establish the Business Baseline

The executive needs one version of reality.

Build the baseline before making major structural decisions.

## Financial

Understand:

- Revenue
- Gross profit
- Gross margin
- Operating expenses
- EBITDA or operating profit
- Cash
- Working capital where relevant
- Budget versus actual
- Forecast
- Revenue concentration
- Major cost drivers

## Commercial

Understand:

- Demand generation
- Qualified pipeline
- Pipeline coverage
- Conversion
- Sales cycle
- Pricing
- New business
- Retention
- Churn
- Expansion
- Forecast accuracy
- Customer concentration

## Customer

Understand:

- Customer segments
- Value proposition
- Retention
- Satisfaction
- Complaints
- Delivery issues
- Strategic accounts
- Customer economics

## Operations

Understand:

- Delivery model
- Capacity
- Productivity
- Quality
- Service levels
- Bottlenecks
- Cost to serve
- Critical dependencies

## Organization

Understand:

- Structure
- Leadership capability
- Accountability
- Decision rights
- Critical vacancies
- Performance issues
- Incentives
- Management cadence
- Cross-functional friction

## Strategy

Understand:

- Markets
- Customers
- Products
- Competitive position
- Strategic priorities
- Current investments
- Major assumptions
- What the organization has deliberately chosen not to do

## Technology & AI

Understand:

- Core systems
- Data quality
- Automation
- Reporting
- Workflow friction
- Technology dependencies
- AI use cases
- Security and governance constraints

The purpose is not exhaustive analysis.

The purpose is to identify what materially explains current performance.

# 05. Listening Architecture

Early conversations should produce evidence.

Use a consistent set of questions.

Ask leaders:

1. What is working?
2. What is not working?
3. Where are we missing plan?
4. Why?
5. What is the biggest constraint on performance?
6. What decision are we avoiding?
7. Where is accountability unclear?
8. What would you change first?
9. What should I not change without understanding it better?
10. What would make the next 90 days successful?

Ask customers:

1. Why do you buy from us?
2. Where do we create the most value?
3. Where do we make your life difficult?
4. What would cause you to leave?
5. What should we improve first?

Look for patterns.

Do not treat individual opinions as facts.

# 07. Build the Constraint Map

Performance problems often have multiple visible symptoms but fewer underlying causes.

Map them.

| Symptom | Root Cause | Business Impact | Evidence | Intervention |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | |
| | | | | |
| | | | | |

Example:

**Symptom:** Revenue below plan

Possible underlying causes:

**Demand → Pipeline Quality → Conversion → Pricing → Capacity → Retention → Execution**

Do not accept the symptom as the diagnosis.

Find the driver.

# 08. Prioritize Ruthlessly

By Day 30, leadership should know what matters most.

Limit the executive agenda to three to five material priorities.

Score potential priorities against:

- Business impact
- Urgency
- Strategic relevance
- Financial impact
- Customer impact
- Execution feasibility
- Leadership capacity
- Cost of delay

Example:

| Priority | Impact | Urgency | Feasibility | Cost of Delay | Executive Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | High / Medium / Low | | | | |
| | | | | | |
| | | | | | |

Then state explicitly:

### We will prioritize:

1.
2.
3.
4.
5.

### We will not prioritize:

1.
2.
3.

Strategy becomes operational when resources stop being spread across everything.

# 09. Quick-Win Filter

Quick wins are useful only when they improve the business.

Do not manufacture visible activity to create the appearance of momentum.

A valid quick win should meet at least one of these conditions:

- Removes a material constraint
- Improves customer experience
- Improves revenue
- Protects margin
- Reduces cost
- Improves cash
- Eliminates unnecessary work
- Clarifies accountability
- Accelerates an important decision
- Reduces material risk

Evaluate:

| Potential Action | Business Impact | Speed | Effort | Reversible? | Execute? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | | |
| | | | | | |

> Speed matters when the action matters.

# 12. Day 0 to 15: Understand

Primary objective:

**Establish the facts.**

Critical actions:

- Confirm mandate
- Align with CEO or Board
- Establish Day-90 outcomes
- Review financial performance
- Review commercial performance
- Review customer evidence
- Review organization
- Review strategy
- Meet key leaders
- Meet selected customers
- Identify immediate risks
- Identify decisions that cannot wait
- Establish initial business baseline

By Day 15, the executive should be able to answer:

**What business did I actually inherit?**

---

# 13. Day 16 to 30: Diagnose & Prioritize

Primary objective:

**Determine what matters most.**

Critical actions:

- Complete organizational diagnostic
- Identify root causes
- Build constraint map
- Validate economics
- Assess leadership capability
- Identify critical organizational gaps
- Confirm three to five priorities
- Stop or defer low-value initiatives
- Define KPI architecture
- Establish decision rights
- Define intervention portfolio
- Align priorities with CEO or Board

By Day 30, the executive should be able to answer:

**What is limiting performance and what are we going to do about it?**

---

# 14. Day 31 to 60: Intervene

Primary objective:

**Change the drivers of performance.**

Critical actions:

- Launch priority interventions
- Assign owners
- Establish Weekly Operating Review
- Implement Monthly Business Review
- Resolve blocked decisions
- Address critical leadership gaps
- Reallocate resources where required
- Correct forecast assumptions
- Implement quick wins
- Track KPI movement
- Review customer and employee signals
- Escalate material constraints

By Day 60, leadership should be able to answer:

**What have we changed, and is the business responding?**

---

# 15. Day 61 to 90: Prove & Institutionalize

Primary objective:

**Demonstrate progress and embed the operating model.**

Critical actions:

- Measure intervention results
- Compare baseline with current state
- Validate forecast
- Review organizational changes
- Close ineffective interventions
- Scale successful interventions
- Confirm leadership ownership
- Embed management cadence
- Confirm decision rights
- Update strategic assumptions
- Define the next 90-day agenda
- Present Day-90 business review

By Day 90, leadership should be able to answer:

**What improved, what remains constrained, and what happens next?**

# 16. Operating Cadence

The 90-day plan requires a management rhythm.

## Weekly

Review:

- Priorities
- KPI exceptions
- Commitments
- Decisions
- Constraints
- Risks
- Actions

## Monthly

Review:

- Financial performance
- Commercial performance
- Forecast
- Intervention impact
- Resource allocation
- Organizational capability
- Major risks

## Day 30

Confirm diagnosis and priorities.

## Day 60

Review intervention effectiveness.

## Day 90

Evaluate outcomes and define the next operating cycle.

The cadence exists to accelerate decisions and execution.

# 19. CEO / Board Update

Executive communication should be concise.

A Day-30, Day-60, or Day-90 update should answer:

### 1. Current State

What is the business situation?

### 2. What We Learned

What evidence changed our understanding?

### 3. Priorities

What are the three to five priorities?

### 4. Performance

What is on plan, at risk, or off plan?

### 5. Interventions

What have we changed?

### 6. Results

What measurable movement has occurred?

### 7. Decisions Required

What requires CEO or Board action?

### 8. Risks

What could materially affect the plan?

### 9. Next 30 Days

What happens next?

The purpose is decision support, not presentation volume.

# Executive Standard

By Day 30, the executive should know:

**What business did I inherit?**

**What is materially limiting performance?**

**What matters most?**

By Day 60:

**What have we changed?**

**Is performance responding?**

**Where are we still constrained?**

By Day 90:

**What improved?**

**What did not?**

**What did we learn?**

**What should happen next?**

A successful first 90 days should create greater clarity, stronger accountability, better decisions, and measurable movement in business performance.
