# GTM & Revenue Operating System

### A practical system for building, diagnosing, and managing predictable revenue growth.

Revenue growth is an output.

Leadership cannot manage the output directly.

It must manage the system that produces it.

A commercial organization should make it possible to understand:

1. Where growth will come from.
2. Which customers and markets matter most.
3. Whether sufficient demand is being created.
4. Whether pipeline is real and sufficient.
5. Where conversion is breaking.
6. Whether pricing supports the economics of the business.
7. Whether customers are being retained and expanded.
8. Whether commercial capacity can support the plan.
9. Whether the forecast can be trusted.
10. What intervention is required when performance deviates.

> **Do not manage the revenue number. Manage the drivers that produce the revenue number.**

---

# The Revenue System

A typical revenue engine can be represented as:

**Market → ICP → Value Proposition → Demand → Qualified Pipeline → Conversion → Revenue → Retention → Expansion**

Supported by:

**People → Process → Data → Technology → Incentives → Management Cadence**

The objective is not to optimize each component independently.

The objective is to create a system capable of producing the required commercial outcome.

# 13. Understand Unit Economics

Growth should create economic value.

Depending on the business model, monitor:

- CAC
- LTV
- LTV:CAC
- CAC payback
- Gross margin
- Contribution margin
- Cost to serve
- Revenue per customer
- Revenue per employee
- Sales productivity

For recurring-revenue businesses:

**LTV:CAC = Customer Lifetime Value / Customer Acquisition Cost**

**CAC Payback = Customer Acquisition Cost / Monthly Gross Profit Contribution**

The specific formula may vary by business model.

The principle does not:

> **Growth that destroys unit economics is not healthy growth.**

# 16. Build a Reliable Forecast

A forecast should represent the most likely commercial outcome based on current evidence.

Do not confuse:

**Target**

with

**Forecast.**

The target is what the organization intends to achieve.

The forecast is what current evidence indicates will happen.

Use:

| Period | Target | Pipeline | Commit | Best Case | Forecast | Gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Month | | | | | | |
| Quarter | | | | | | |

Forecast quality depends on:

- Stage discipline
- Opportunity qualification
- Close-date accuracy
- Historical conversion
- Seller judgment
- Manager inspection
- Deal-level evidence

Measure forecast accuracy over time.

A forecast that repeatedly changes at the end of the period is not a forecasting system.

# 17. Diagnose Forecast Risk

For material deals, review:

| Deal | Value | Stage | Expected Close | Evidence | Risk | Required Action |
|---|---:|---|---|---|---|---|

Typical risks include:

- No confirmed economic buyer
- Weak business case
- No compelling event
- Unclear decision process
- Budget not confirmed
- Legal or procurement delay
- Competitive displacement
- Product gap
- Single-threaded relationship
- Unrealistic close date

Do not ask:

**Do you think this deal will close?**

Ask:

**What evidence supports the close date?**

# 23. Establish the Revenue Management Cadence

Different commercial questions require different horizons.

| Cadence | Purpose | Typical Horizon | Core Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weekly Revenue Review | Pipeline, forecast, constraints | Current month / quarter | Actions and decisions |
| Monthly Commercial Review | Performance and economics | 30 to 90 days | Corrective interventions |
| Quarterly GTM Review | Strategy and resource allocation | 3 to 12 months | Strategic adjustments |
| Annual Planning | Markets, capacity, investment | 12 to 36 months | Revenue plan |

Do not turn every commercial meeting into pipeline inspection.

Each meeting should answer a different management question.

# 25. Revenue Root-Cause Tree

When revenue is below plan, diagnose systematically.

Start with:

**Revenue Gap**

Then inspect:

**Demand**
- Insufficient demand
- Poor channel performance
- Wrong audience
- High acquisition cost

**Pipeline**
- Insufficient pipeline
- Poor qualification
- Pipeline aging
- Incorrect stage classification

**Conversion**
- Low win rate
- Weak value proposition
- Product gap
- Competitive losses
- Poor sales execution

**Deal Value**
- Lower ACV
- Excessive discounting
- Product mix
- Segment mix

**Timing**
- Longer sales cycle
- Procurement delays
- Implementation constraints
- Seasonality

**Retention**
- Churn
- Downgrades
- Renewal delays

**Expansion**
- Weak account penetration
- Limited cross-sell
- Low product adoption

Do not jump from revenue variance directly to action.

Diagnose the driver first.

# Executive Standard

At any point, commercial leadership should be able to answer:

**Where will growth come from?**

**Are we creating enough qualified demand?**

**Do we have enough real pipeline?**

**Where is conversion breaking?**

**Are we winning the right customers?**

**Are our unit economics healthy?**

**Can we trust the forecast?**

**Where is revenue at risk?**

**Are we retaining and expanding customers?**

**What is constraining growth?**

**What intervention is required?**

**Who owns it?**

**When will we know whether it worked?**

If leadership cannot answer these questions consistently, the revenue engine is not under control.
