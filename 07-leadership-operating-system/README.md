# Leadership Operating System

### A practical system for turning a group of senior leaders into an accountable executive team.

Strong organizations do not depend on individual leadership heroics.

They create clarity around priorities, accountability, decisions, performance, talent, and execution.

The leadership team exists to run the business as one system.

A Leadership Operating System should answer seven questions:

1. What outcomes does the leadership team collectively own?
2. Who owns what?
3. Where are decisions made?
4. How is performance reviewed?
5. How are disagreements resolved?
6. How are leadership commitments enforced?
7. How does the team improve its ability to execute?

> **A leadership team is effective when the organization can execute through it.**

---

# The Leadership System

The operating logic is:

**Strategy → Priorities → Executive Accountability → Decisions → Commitments → Execution → Performance → Learning**

Leadership effectiveness should ultimately appear in business performance.

The objective is not executive-team harmony.

The objective is coordinated execution.

---

# 01. Define the Leadership Mandate

Every leadership team should understand why it exists.

Clarify:

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What does this leadership team collectively own? | |
| What business outcomes must it deliver? | |
| Which decisions belong here? | |
| Which decisions should remain below this level? | |
| What information must this team review? | |
| What behaviors are required? | |
| What behaviors are unacceptable? | |
| How will leadership effectiveness be measured? | |

A leadership team should not become a reporting forum.

It should exist to:

- Set direction
- Allocate resources
- Resolve material constraints
- Make cross-functional decisions
- Manage enterprise performance
- Build organizational capability
- Develop leaders
- Protect the long-term health of the business

---

# 02. Establish Enterprise Priorities

Functional priorities should follow enterprise priorities.

Leadership should identify the limited number of outcomes that matter most.

Typically:

**3 to 5 enterprise priorities**

For each priority define:

| Priority | Business Outcome | KPI | Target | Executive Owner | Review Cadence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | | |

Then ask every executive:

**What must your function deliver for this enterprise priority to succeed?**

This creates alignment between enterprise objectives and functional execution.

Avoid a leadership model where every executive optimizes their own department while the company misses its objectives.

> **Enterprise performance comes before functional optimization.**

---

# 03. Clarify Executive Accountability

Every material business outcome requires one clearly accountable executive.

Use:

| Outcome | Accountable Executive | Supporting Leaders | KPI | Target | Decision Authority |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | | |

Accountability means:

- The outcome is explicit
- The owner is explicit
- Authority is sufficient
- Resources are understood
- Dependencies are visible
- Performance is measurable
- Review dates are established

Avoid shared accountability for critical outcomes.

Multiple executives may contribute.

One executive should remain accountable.

---

# 04. Define Decision Rights

Leadership teams slow down when decision authority is unclear.

For material recurring decisions, define:

| Decision | Decision Owner | Contributors | Evidence Required | Escalation Trigger | Review |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | | |

Separate:

**Input**

from:

**Recommendation**

from:

**Decision authority**

from:

**Execution ownership**

Not everyone who contributes to a decision needs to approve it.

The objective is sufficient input with clear authority.

> **Push decisions to the lowest competent level. Escalate material constraints, not routine responsibility.**

---

# 05. Build the Executive Scorecard

The leadership team needs one view of enterprise performance.

Avoid combining every departmental metric into a large executive dashboard.

The executive scorecard should focus on the measures required to understand and manage the business.

Typical categories:

## Financial

- Revenue
- Growth
- Gross profit
- Gross margin
- Operating expenses
- EBITDA or operating profit
- Cash
- Forecast

## Commercial

- Demand
- Qualified pipeline
- Pipeline coverage
- Conversion
- New business
- Pricing
- Retention
- Expansion

## Customer

- Retention
- Satisfaction
- Service performance
- Complaints
- Strategic account health

## Operations

- Capacity
- Productivity
- Quality
- Delivery
- Utilization
- Cost to serve

## People

- Critical vacancies
- Leadership gaps
- Regrettable attrition
- Productivity
- Succession risk
- Critical capability gaps

## Strategic

- Strategic initiative progress
- Market expansion
- Product adoption
- Transformation outcomes
- Technology and AI priorities

Every KPI should have:

**Definition → Target → Actual → Trend → Forecast → Owner → Action Threshold**

# 06. Run Leadership by Exception

Executive attention is limited.

Do not spend equal time on everything.

Leadership should focus disproportionate attention on:

- Material deviations
- Strategic risks
- Cross-functional constraints
- Major opportunities
- Resource conflicts
- Decisions
- Failed commitments
- Forecast changes

Use:

| Issue | Business Impact | Evidence | Owner | Decision Required | Required By |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | | |

If performance is on plan and no executive intervention is required, acknowledge it and move on.

The leadership meeting should concentrate on what needs leadership.

# 07. Establish the Leadership Cadence

Different management questions require different horizons.

| Cadence | Primary Purpose | Typical Horizon | Core Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weekly Executive Review | Execution and constraints | 7 to 30 days | Decisions and actions |
| Monthly Business Review | Business performance | 30 to 90 days | Corrective interventions |
| Quarterly Strategy Review | Strategy and resources | 3 to 12 months | Strategic adjustments |
| Talent Review | Leadership capability | 3 to 18 months | People decisions |
| Annual Strategy Cycle | Direction and capital allocation | 12 to 36 months | Strategic plan |

Do not turn every meeting into the same meeting with different names.

Each cadence should have a distinct management purpose.

---

# 08. Design the Weekly Executive Review

The weekly executive review should keep execution moving.

Recommended duration:

**60 to 90 minutes**

Recommended sequence:

## 1. Critical Business Changes

What materially changed since the previous review?

## 2. Enterprise Priorities

Are the critical priorities on plan?

## 3. KPI Exceptions

Which measures require leadership attention?

## 4. Open Commitments

What was promised?

Was it delivered?

## 5. Decisions Required

What decisions are blocking execution?

## 6. Cross-Functional Constraints

Where does one function require another function to act?

## 7. Risks

What could materially affect the plan?

## 8. New Commitments

What happens next?

Every new commitment requires:

**Action → Owner → Due Date → Expected Outcome**

# 09. Create a Leadership Commitment System

Leadership credibility depends on follow-through.

Track material commitments.

| Commitment | Owner | Date Agreed | Due Date | Status | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | | |

Use simple statuses:

- Open
- Completed
- At Risk
- Overdue
- Cancelled

Do not allow commitments to disappear between meetings.

Review overdue commitments explicitly.

Ask:

**What prevented delivery?**

**What is the business impact?**

**What happens now?**

Repeated missed commitments indicate a management problem.

---

# 10. Manage Cross-Functional Dependencies

Many execution failures occur between functions.

Typical examples:

- Sales depends on Marketing
- Marketing depends on Product
- Product depends on Technology
- Operations depends on Sales forecasting
- Finance depends on operating data
- International markets depend on central functions
- Customer success depends on product quality

Track material dependencies.

| Dependency | Requesting Function | Delivering Function | Required Outcome | Due Date | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | | |

Leadership should intervene when cross-functional constraints materially affect enterprise performance.

Do not allow functional boundaries to become an excuse for missed outcomes.

---

# 11. Make Resource Allocation Explicit

Strategy becomes real when resources move.

Leadership should regularly review whether resources match priorities.

Review:

- Capital
- Headcount
- Leadership attention
- Technology capacity
- Marketing investment
- Sales capacity
- Product resources
- External partners

Use:

| Priority | Required Resources | Current Resources | Gap | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | |

Then ask:

**What should receive more?**

**What should receive less?**

**What should stop?**

A priority without resources is an intention.

---

# 12. Establish Leadership Standards

Leadership expectations should be explicit.

A senior leader should:

- Understand the business beyond their own function
- Know the numbers
- Surface problems early
- Bring evidence
- Make recommendations
- Make decisions within their authority
- Own outcomes
- Deliver commitments
- Resolve cross-functional constraints
- Develop capable leaders
- Protect enterprise interests

The standard should apply equally across the executive team.

---

# 13. Surface Problems Early

Bad news becomes expensive when it travels slowly.

Leadership should create an operating environment where material problems surface quickly.

When escalating a problem, bring:

1. What happened?
2. What is the evidence?
3. What is the business impact?
4. What caused it?
5. What has already been attempted?
6. What is the recommendation?
7. What decision or support is required?
8. By when?

Avoid escalation that simply transfers responsibility upward.

> **Escalate with evidence, diagnosis, and a recommendation.**

---

# 14. Manage Executive Conflict

Disagreement is normal.

Unresolved disagreement is expensive.

For material disagreements:

## Step 1. Define the Decision

What exactly must be decided?

## Step 2. Separate Facts From Assumptions

What do we know?

What are we assuming?

## Step 3. Define the Trade-Off

What are the competing objectives?

## Step 4. Identify Decision Authority

Who owns the decision?

## Step 5. Decide

Make the decision explicit.

## Step 6. Commit

Once decided, the leadership team executes the decision.

Document material decisions:

| Decision | Options Considered | Evidence | Decision | Owner | Review Date |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | | |

The objective is not consensus on every issue.

The objective is effective decision-making followed by coordinated execution.

---

# 15. Build Leadership Capability

A leadership team should become stronger over time.

Assess each critical leadership role against:

- Strategic thinking
- Business judgment
- Financial understanding
- Commercial understanding
- Execution
- Decision quality
- People leadership
- Cross-functional leadership
- Change leadership
- Technology and AI fluency
- Communication
- Talent development

Use:

| Leader / Role | Strengths | Critical Gap | Business Impact | Development Action | Review Date |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | | |

Development should connect to business requirements.

Avoid generic leadership development disconnected from the operating mandate.

---

# 16. Review Leadership Capacity

A capable executive can still fail if the role exceeds available capacity.

Review:

- Number of priorities
- Span of control
- Organizational complexity
- Decision load
- Operational workload
- Transformation responsibilities
- Geographic scope
- Critical vacancies
- Required travel
- Management support

Use:

| Executive Role | Critical Priorities | Capacity Risk | Constraint | Required Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | |

Do not continuously add responsibilities without reviewing capacity.

---

# 17. Build Succession Depth

Critical roles should not depend on one individual.

Identify:

| Critical Role | Current Leader | Successor Ready Now | Successor 12 Months | Risk | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | | |

For critical roles ask:

**What happens if this person leaves tomorrow?**

Leadership resilience requires succession depth.

---

# 18. Review the Organization Through Leaders

Leadership quality should appear below the executive team.

For each executive area, ask:

- Are priorities clear?
- Are decisions being made at the right level?
- Are managers accountable?
- Are commitments delivered?
- Are problems surfaced early?
- Is performance improving?
- Are capable leaders being developed?
- Can the function operate without constant executive intervention?

The objective is not to create indispensable executives.

The objective is to create capable organizations.

> **A leader scales when the organization can perform without requiring their constant intervention.**

---

# 19. Conduct Quarterly Leadership Reviews

Once per quarter, review the effectiveness of the leadership system itself.

Ask:

## Strategy

Are enterprise priorities clear?

## Performance

Are we delivering expected outcomes?

## Decisions

Are decisions being made quickly enough?

## Accountability

Is ownership clear?

## Execution

Are commitments being delivered?

## Collaboration

Are cross-functional constraints being resolved?

## Capability

Do we have the leadership capability required for the strategy?

## Succession

Where are we dependent on individual leaders?

## Capacity

Where is leadership overloaded?

## Learning

What should we change about how we run the company?

The leadership system itself should improve.

# Close

Before leaving the meeting:

**Are enterprise priorities clear?**

**Do we know where performance is off plan?**

**Have we identified the cause?**

**Are required decisions explicit?**

**Does every decision have an owner?**

**Are cross-functional constraints assigned?**

**Does every commitment have one owner and a deadline?**

**Do we know what happens next?**

If these questions cannot be answered, the leadership meeting has not completed its job.

# Executive Standard

At any point, the leadership team should know:

**What matters most.**

**How the business is performing.**

**Where performance is deviating.**

**What is causing the deviation.**

**What decisions have been made.**

**What decisions remain unresolved.**

**Who owns each material outcome.**

**Which commitments are overdue.**

**Where cross-functional execution is constrained.**

**Where leadership capability is insufficient.**

**What happens next.**
