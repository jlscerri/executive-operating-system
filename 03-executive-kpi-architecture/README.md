# Executive KPI Architecture

### A practical system for connecting strategy, business performance, and executive action.

Organizations rarely suffer from a lack of data.

They suffer from too many metrics, unclear ownership, inconsistent definitions, and dashboards that describe performance without helping leadership manage it.

An executive KPI system should answer five questions:

1. Are we delivering the expected business outcomes?
2. What is driving those outcomes?
3. Where is performance deviating?
4. What requires executive attention?
5. What action or decision is required?

> **A KPI earns executive attention when it can change a decision.**

---

# The Executive KPI System

Executive KPIs should connect:

**Strategy → Outcomes → Drivers → Health → Risks → Decisions → Actions**

The objective is not to create the largest dashboard.

The objective is to create the smallest set of measures that allows leadership to understand the business and act.

---

# 01. Start With Strategy

Do not begin by asking:

**What can we measure?**

Begin with:

**What must be true for the strategy to succeed?**

For each strategic priority, define:

| Strategic Priority | Required Outcome | Critical Driver | KPI | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
|  |  |  |  |  |
|  |  |  |  |  |
|  |  |  |  |  |

Every material strategic priority should have measurable evidence of progress.

If a KPI cannot be connected to a business outcome or strategic priority, question why leadership is reviewing it.

---

# 02. Four KPI Categories

A useful executive scorecard separates metrics into four categories.

## Outcomes

**What results are we producing?**

Examples:

- Revenue
- Gross profit
- Gross margin
- EBITDA / operating profit
- Cash
- New business
- Retention
- Market share

Outcome metrics tell leadership what happened.

They rarely explain why.

---

## Drivers

**What creates those outcomes?**

Examples:

- Qualified pipeline
- Conversion
- Average selling price
- Sales productivity
- Utilization
- Customer acquisition
- Delivery productivity
- Capacity
- Pricing
- Product mix

Driver metrics help leadership understand what is likely to move future outcomes.

---

## Health Indicators

**Can the organization sustain performance?**

Examples:

- Customer retention
- Employee retention
- Customer satisfaction
- Quality
- Delivery capacity
- Critical vacancies
- Leadership vacancies
- Employee productivity
- Service levels

A business can hit today's number while weakening tomorrow's operating capability.

Health indicators expose that risk.

---

## Strategic Indicators

**Are we progressing against the strategy?**

Examples:

- New market penetration
- Product adoption
- Strategic customer acquisition
- Geographic expansion
- Channel development
- New revenue mix
- Transformation milestones
- Capability development

Strategic indicators prevent management from optimizing current performance while losing sight of future value creation.

---

# 03. Leading and Lagging Indicators

Leadership needs both.

## Lagging Indicators

These confirm results already produced.

Examples:

- Revenue
- EBITDA
- Gross margin
- Cash
- Churn
- New business closed

They answer:

**What happened?**

## Leading Indicators

These provide evidence about future performance.

Examples:

- Qualified pipeline
- Pipeline coverage
- Conversion by stage
- Sales velocity
- Customer usage
- Capacity utilization
- Hiring progress
- Delivery backlog
- Renewal risk

They answer:

**What is likely to happen next?**

A strong executive scorecard should make it difficult for leadership to be surprised by the month-end result.

---

# 04. KPI Definition Standard

Every critical KPI should have a clear operating definition.

| Field | Definition |
|---|---|
| KPI | What is being measured |
| Purpose | Why the metric matters |
| Formula | How it is calculated |
| Target | Expected performance |
| Threshold | When intervention is required |
| Owner | Executive accountable for performance |
| Source | Authoritative data source |
| Frequency | How often it is updated |
| Trend | Direction of performance |
| Forecast | Expected future position |
| Action | Required response when outside tolerance |

Without these definitions, organizations can spend management meetings debating the number instead of managing the business.

# 06. Financial Architecture

Leadership should understand the economic engine of the business.

Typical executive measures include:

| KPI | Question |
|---|---|
| Revenue | Are we producing expected growth? |
| Gross Profit | Are we creating sufficient economic value? |
| Gross Margin | Is the revenue economically attractive? |
| Operating Expenses | Are resources aligned with priorities? |
| EBITDA / Operating Profit | Is growth translating into profitability? |
| Cash | Can the business fund its commitments and strategy? |
| Working Capital | Is operational growth consuming excessive cash? |
| Forecast | Where are we likely to finish? |

The purpose is not financial reporting.

The purpose is understanding the economics behind management decisions.

---

# 07. Commercial Architecture

Revenue should be managed as a system.

A typical commercial chain is:

**Demand → Qualified Pipeline → Conversion → Revenue → Retention → Expansion**

Example:

| Stage | KPI | Executive Question |
|---|---|---|
| Demand | Qualified opportunities | Are we creating sufficient demand? |
| Pipeline | Pipeline value / coverage | Is there enough future revenue opportunity? |
| Conversion | Conversion rate | Are opportunities becoming customers? |
| Revenue | New business / bookings | Are we producing the expected result? |
| Retention | Renewal / churn | Are we keeping the value we create? |
| Expansion | Upsell / cross-sell | Are existing customers growing? |

When revenue misses plan, leadership should identify where the commercial system is breaking.

---

# 08. Operational Architecture

Operational KPIs should expose the organization's ability to deliver.

Depending on the business model:

- Productivity
- Utilization
- Capacity
- Cost to serve
- Quality
- Delivery time
- Service level
- Backlog
- Customer issues
- Rework
- Unit economics

The objective is to connect operating performance to customer outcomes and economics.

---

# 09. Customer Architecture

Customer metrics should indicate both current experience and future economic risk.

Possible measures:

- Customer retention
- Churn
- Renewal rate
- Customer satisfaction
- NPS, where useful
- Product or service usage
- Complaints
- Escalations
- Expansion
- Revenue concentration

Leadership should be able to answer:

**Are customers receiving enough value to remain, grow, and advocate for the business?**

---

# 10. People & Leadership Architecture

People metrics should help leadership understand organizational capability.

Possible measures:

- Critical vacancies
- Leadership vacancies
- Employee retention
- Regrettable attrition
- Time to hire
- Productivity
- Capacity
- Performance distribution
- Succession coverage
- Critical capability gaps

Avoid turning the executive dashboard into an HR report.

Track people measures that materially affect execution.

---

# 11. KPI Trees

Important outcomes should be decomposed into their underlying drivers.

Example:

**Revenue**

↓

**Customers × Average Revenue per Customer**

Customers may then depend on:

**Demand × Qualification × Conversion × Retention**

Average revenue may depend on:

**Price × Product Mix × Expansion**

This creates a management tree.

When the outcome moves, leadership can investigate the drivers rather than speculate.

---

# 12. Variance Logic

Every material variance should move through the same sequence:

**Target → Actual → Variance → Cause → Forecast Impact → Intervention → Owner → Review Date**

Example:

| KPI | Target | Actual | Variance | Root Cause | Forecast Impact | Intervention | Owner | Review |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
|  |  |  |  |  |  |  |  |  |

Do not accept:

**Revenue is below plan.**

That is an observation.

Management needs to know:

**Why is revenue below plan, what happens if nothing changes, and what intervention will change the outcome?**

---

# 13. Thresholds and Exception Management

Not every variance deserves executive attention.

Define thresholds.

Example:

| Status | Meaning | Response |
|---|---|---|
| On Plan | Within agreed tolerance | Continue |
| At Risk | Material risk to target | Management intervention |
| Off Plan | Target unlikely without corrective action | Executive decision / escalation |

Executive attention is scarce.

Use it where intervention can materially change the outcome.

> **Manage by exception, not by dashboard.**

---

# 14. KPI Ownership

Every critical KPI requires one accountable owner.

The owner should be able to explain:

1. Current performance
2. Variance to target
3. Root cause
4. Forecast
5. Required intervention
6. Expected impact
7. Review date

Ownership does not mean one person controls every driver.

It means one person is accountable for understanding performance and mobilizing the response.

---

# 15. Review Frequency

Different KPIs require different management horizons.

| Frequency | Typical Focus |
|---|---|
| Daily | High-velocity operating exceptions |
| Weekly | Drivers, execution, pipeline, delivery |
| Monthly | Business outcomes, economics, forecast |
| Quarterly | Strategy, resources, structural performance |

Do not review every metric at every meeting.

Match the metric to the decision horizon.

---

# 16. Executive Dashboard Design

A useful executive dashboard should allow leadership to understand the state of the business quickly.

For each critical KPI show:

**Target | Actual | Variance | Trend | Forecast | Status | Owner | Required Action**

Avoid:

- Excessive metrics
- Decorative charts
- Metrics without targets
- Metrics without owners
- Metrics without definitions
- Metrics without action thresholds
- Reporting numbers that nobody uses to make decisions

A dashboard is useful only when it improves management.

# Executive Standard

At any point, leadership should be able to answer:

**What outcomes matter most?**

**Are we on plan?**

**What is driving performance?**

**Where are we deviating?**

**Why?**

**What does the current evidence imply about the forecast?**

**What requires intervention?**

**Who owns the response?**

**When will we know whether it worked?**

If the KPI system cannot answer these questions, it is reporting data rather than managing performance.

---

# Operating Principle

> **Measure what matters.**
>
> **Understand what moves it.**
>
> **Intervene where performance can change.**
>
> **Assign ownership.**
>
> **Review whether the intervention worked.**
