# Monthly Business Review

### An executive management system for understanding business performance, challenging the forecast, and deciding where leadership must intervene.

The Monthly Business Review is not a longer Weekly Operating Review.

It asks a different question:

> **Is the business performing as expected, why or why not, and what should leadership change?**

The review connects financial performance, commercial performance, customers, operations, people, strategic priorities, and forward outlook.

Its purpose is to convert business performance into management action.

---

## Purpose

The Monthly Business Review should enable leadership to answer:

1. What happened?
2. How did actual performance compare with plan?
3. What materially explains the variance?
4. Which trends matter?
5. What is likely to happen next?
6. Where are the largest risks and opportunities?
7. What intervention is required?
8. What should change in the forecast, priorities, resources, or execution?

The objective is not to explain every number.

The objective is to understand the economics and operating performance of the business well enough to make better decisions.

---

# Operating Standard

**Frequency:** Monthly

**Duration:** 90 to 120 minutes

**Participants:** CEO / GM, Finance, Commercial, Operations, Customer, People, and relevant functional leaders

**Meeting owner:** CEO, GM, COO, or designated business leader

**Financial owner:** CFO, Finance Director, or Finance lead

**Pre-read deadline:** Ideally 48 hours before the review

**Primary horizon:** Previous month, year-to-date, and forward 90 days

**Primary outputs:**

- Performance diagnosis
- Forecast changes
- Corrective interventions
- Resource decisions
- Strategic escalations
- Named owners
- Measurable outcomes
- Review dates

---

# The Core Management Logic

The Monthly Business Review follows a simple sequence:

**Actual → Plan → Variance → Cause → Forecast → Intervention → Owner → Outcome**

Do not stop at variance.

A variance without diagnosis is information.

A diagnosis without action is observation.

The management value comes from determining what should happen next.

---

# Before the Meeting

The pre-read should contain the information required to make decisions.

Do not use meeting time to discover numbers that could have been reviewed beforehand.

## Required Inputs

### 1. Financial Performance

At minimum:

- Revenue
- Gross profit
- Gross margin
- Operating expenses
- EBITDA or relevant operating profit measure
- Cash position
- Working capital, where relevant
- Actual vs budget
- Actual vs forecast
- Year-to-date performance

### 2. Commercial Performance

Depending on the business model:

- Pipeline
- New business
- Bookings
- ARR / MRR
- Conversion
- Win rate
- Average contract value
- Sales cycle
- Pricing
- Renewals
- Expansion
- Churn
- Channel performance
- Market performance

### 3. Customer Performance

Relevant indicators may include:

- Customer acquisition
- Retention
- Churn
- NPS / satisfaction
- Service levels
- Customer complaints
- Adoption
- Usage
- Delivery performance
- Concentration risk

### 4. Operational Performance

Relevant indicators may include:

- Capacity
- Productivity
- Utilization
- Cost per unit
- Quality
- Delivery
- Cycle time
- Backlog
- SLA performance
- Operational incidents

### 5. People & Organization

Review only metrics that materially affect business performance.

Examples:

- Headcount vs plan
- Critical vacancies
- Productivity
- Attrition
- Leadership gaps
- Capacity constraints
- Capability gaps
- Organizational risks

### 6. Strategic Priorities

For each enterprise priority:

- Intended outcome
- Current status
- Progress against milestone
- Business impact
- Material constraint
- Decision required

### 7. Forecast

Leadership should receive an updated forward view.

At minimum:

- Current forecast
- Previous forecast
- Budget / plan
- Material changes
- Key assumptions
- Risks
- Upside opportunities

# The Monthly Business Review

## 01. Executive Performance Summary

**10 minutes**

Start with the business, not the functions.

Answer:

- Where did we land?
- What was materially different from plan?
- What improved?
- What deteriorated?
- What changed in the outlook?
- What requires leadership attention?

Use a simple executive status:

**On Plan | At Risk | Off Plan**

The opening should establish the few issues that matter most.

---

## 02. Financial Performance

**20 minutes**

Review financial performance as an integrated system.

### P&L View

| Metric | Plan | Actual | Variance | YTD Plan | YTD Actual | Forecast |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue |  |  |  |  |  |  |
| Gross Profit |  |  |  |  |  |  |
| Gross Margin % |  |  |  |  |  |  |
| Operating Expenses |  |  |  |  |  |  |
| EBITDA / Operating Profit |  |  |  |  |  |  |
| Cash |  |  |  |  |  |  |

For every material variance, ask:

**What happened?**

**Why?**

**Is it temporary or structural?**

**Was it anticipated?**

**What happens if we do nothing?**

**What intervention is required?**

Do not accept “revenue was below plan” as a diagnosis.

Find the driver.

# Variance Bridge

Material financial variance should be decomposed into its underlying drivers.

For revenue, this may include:

**Volume + Price + Mix + Conversion + Retention + Timing + Market**

For margin:

**Price + Product Mix + Delivery Cost + Utilization + Productivity + Supplier Cost**

For operating expenses:

**Headcount + Hiring Timing + Marketing + Technology + Professional Services + Other**

Example:

| Driver | Impact | Explanation | Structural / Temporary | Response |
|---|---:|---|---|---|

| Mix |  |  |  |  |
| Timing |  |  |  |  |

The purpose is to move from:

**We missed the number**

to:

**We understand what moved the number.**

## 03. Commercial Engine

**20 minutes**

Evaluate whether the revenue engine can produce the expected future performance.

Review the funnel from demand to revenue.

A typical structure:

**Demand → Pipeline → Conversion → Revenue → Retention → Expansion**

| Metric | Target | Actual | Previous Month | Trend | Forecast | Status |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|---|
| Qualified Pipeline |  |  |  | ↑ ↓ → |  |  |
| Pipeline Coverage |  |  |  | ↑ ↓ → |  |  |
| Win Rate |  |  |  | ↑ ↓ → |  |  |
| Conversion |  |  |  | ↑ ↓ → |  |  |
| Average Deal Value |  |  |  | ↑ ↓ → |  |  |
| Sales Cycle |  |  |  | ↑ ↓ → |  |  |
| Retention |  |  |  | ↑ ↓ → |  |  |

Ask:

- Is pipeline sufficient to support the forecast?
- Where is conversion changing?
- Are we winning the right customers?
- Is pricing improving or deteriorating?
- Are sales cycles changing?
- Where are deals being lost?
- Are acquisition economics sustainable?
- Is retention supporting growth?
- Which markets, segments, products, or channels are outperforming?

Do not manage revenue only through the final revenue number.

Manage the drivers that create it.

---

## 04. Customer & Operations

**15 minutes**

Revenue quality depends on what happens after the sale.

Review the operational system required to deliver the customer promise.

Focus on exceptions involving:

- Customer retention
- Service quality
- Delivery
- Capacity
- Productivity
- Utilization
- Cost
- Backlog
- Customer complaints
- Operational risk

Ask:

> **Can the operating model sustain the performance we are asking the commercial engine to create?**

Growth without delivery capacity creates future performance problems.

---

## 05. People & Organizational Capacity

**10 minutes**

Do not turn this into an HR report.

Review organizational issues that materially affect execution.

Focus on:

- Critical leadership vacancies
- Capacity constraints
- Performance gaps
- Capability gaps
- Productivity
- Organizational bottlenecks
- High-risk attrition
- Hiring required by the operating plan

Ask:

> **Do we have the leadership, capability, and capacity required to deliver the plan?**

If not:

- What capability is missing?
- What business outcome is affected?
- What intervention is required?
- By when?

---

## 06. Strategic Priorities

**10 minutes**

Review progress against the small number of enterprise priorities.

| Priority | Intended Outcome | Status | Business Impact | Constraint | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
|  |  |  |  |  |  |

Ask:

- Are the priorities producing the intended business impact?
- Has anything changed that challenges the original assumption?
- Are resources still allocated correctly?
- Should anything be accelerated?
- Should anything be stopped?

Activity is not strategic progress.

Measure outcomes.

---

## 07. Forecast & Outlook

**15 minutes**

The forecast is a management instrument, not a promise.

Its purpose is to provide the best current view of likely performance.

Compare:

**Budget → Previous Forecast → Current Forecast → Risk Case → Upside Case**

### Forecast View

| Metric | Budget | Previous Forecast | Current Forecast | Risk Case | Upside Case |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue |  |  |  |  |  |
| Gross Margin |  |  |  |  |  |
| EBITDA / Operating Profit |  |  |  |  |  |
| Cash |  |  |  |  |  |

For every material forecast change:

- What changed?
- Why?
- Which assumption changed?
- What evidence supports the new view?
- What can management still influence?

> **Do not protect the forecast. Improve its accuracy.**

# Corrective Intervention Framework

When performance is materially off plan, define the intervention explicitly.

## Performance Gap

What outcome is below expectation?

## Root Cause

What evidence explains the gap?

## Intervention

What specifically will change?

## Owner

Who is accountable?

## Expected Impact

What measurable result should the intervention create?

## Timing

When should the impact become visible?

## Review Date

When will leadership evaluate whether the intervention worked?

Use:

| Performance Gap | Root Cause | Intervention | Owner | Expected Impact | Review Date |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
|  |  |  |  |  |  |

Avoid vague actions such as:

“Improve sales.”

“Focus on costs.”

“Push harder.”

“Monitor closely.”

Management interventions must describe what will actually change.

# Resource Allocation Review

Resources should follow priorities and economics.

Monthly review should challenge whether current allocation still makes sense.

Consider:

- People
- Capital
- Marketing investment
- Technology
- Management attention
- External partners
- Inventory
- Capacity

Ask:

1. Where are resources producing strong returns?
2. Where are resources underperforming?
3. Where are we capacity constrained?
4. What should receive more investment?
5. What should receive less?
6. What should stop?

> **A priority without resources is an intention.**

# What Should Not Be in This Meeting

The Monthly Business Review should not become:

- A collection of functional presentations
- A review of every KPI
- A detailed project meeting
- A retrospective with no forward view
- A defense of the budget
- A forum for explaining poor performance without action
- A meeting where finance owns the business numbers and everyone else observes

Business performance belongs to the leadership team.

Finance provides integrity to the numbers.

Leadership owns the outcome.

---

# Executive Output

At the end of the Monthly Business Review, leadership should be able to state clearly:

**Where the business landed.**

**Why performance differed from plan.**

**Which trends matter.**

**What the current forecast says.**

**Which assumptions changed.**

**Where the largest risks are.**

**Where the largest opportunities are.**

**What interventions have been agreed.**

**Where resources will change.**

**Who owns each response.**

**When leadership will know whether it worked.**

Before leaving the review, leadership should confirm:

**Do we understand the performance?**

**Do we understand the causes?**

**Do we believe the forecast?**

**Have we addressed the material risks?**

**Have we identified the meaningful opportunities?**

**Have we made the required decisions?**

**Have resources been aligned accordingly?**

**Does every intervention have an owner?**
