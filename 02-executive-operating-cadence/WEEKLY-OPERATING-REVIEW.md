# Weekly Operating Review

### A leadership system for identifying deviations, removing constraints, making decisions, and keeping execution moving.

The Weekly Operating Review is not a status meeting.

Its purpose is to determine where execution requires leadership intervention.

Teams should arrive knowing the numbers, commitments, and material issues.

Leadership time is used to understand deviations, resolve constraints, make decisions, and establish clear ownership.

> **Review performance. Diagnose exceptions. Make decisions. Assign ownership.**

---

## Purpose

The Weekly Operating Review should answer five questions:

1. Are we delivering the priorities?
2. Where are we off plan?
3. Why?
4. What requires a decision or intervention?
5. Who owns what happens next?

The meeting succeeds when the organization leaves with greater clarity and fewer unresolved constraints.

---

## Operating Standard

**Frequency:** Weekly

**Duration:** 60 to 90 minutes

**Participants:** CEO / GM and relevant functional leaders

**Meeting owner:** CEO, GM, COO, or designated operating leader

**Pre-read deadline:** Ideally 24 hours before the meeting

**Primary horizon:** Current week through next 30 days

**Primary outputs:**

- Decisions
- Corrective actions
- Escalations
- Resource changes
- Named owners
- Deadlines

---

# Before the Meeting

The meeting should begin before people enter the room.

Each function updates its relevant information before the review.

## Required Inputs

### 1. Priorities

For each critical priority:

- Target
- Current status
- Owner
- Milestone
- Risk
- Required decision, if any

### 2. KPI Exceptions

Do not review every metric with equal attention.

Surface:

- Material deviations from target
- Significant week-on-week changes
- Emerging risks
- Unexpected positive performance
- Metrics requiring leadership action

### 3. Commitments

Review commitments made during the previous operating review.

Each commitment should show:

| Action | Owner | Due Date | Status | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Example | Name | Date | On Track / At Risk / Missed | Result |

### 4. Decision Requests

Any issue requiring leadership intervention should arrive with:

**Decision required**

What specifically needs to be decided?

**Evidence**

What facts or data matter?

**Options**

What realistic choices exist?

**Recommendation**

What does the accountable leader recommend?

**Timing**

When must the decision be made?

# The 60-Minute Weekly Operating Review

## 01. Open Commitments

**10 minutes**

Start with commitments from the previous meeting.

Ask:

- What was completed?
- What was not completed?
- Why?
- Does anything require escalation?

Avoid lengthy explanations.

The objective is accountability and closure.

---

## 02. Business Pulse

**10 minutes**

Review only the indicators required to understand the current state of the business.

Typical areas may include:

- Revenue
- Pipeline
- Conversion
- Margin
- Cash
- Customer performance
- Delivery
- Quality
- Productivity
- People
- Critical strategic indicators

Use a simple status:

**On Track | At Risk | Off Track**

Ask:

> **What changed materially since last week?**

Do not turn the section into a presentation.

---

## 03. Priority Review

**15 minutes**

Review the small number of priorities that materially affect current performance.

For each priority:

**Target**

What are we trying to achieve?

**Current State**

Where are we now?

**Gap**

What is different from plan?

**Cause**

Why does the gap exist?

**Response**

What are we doing about it?

**Owner**

Who is accountable?

Focus attention on exceptions.

Items that are on plan should move quickly.

---

## 04. Constraints & Decisions

**20 minutes**

This is the core of the meeting.

Discuss issues that cannot be solved effectively within the normal operating structure.

A constraint may involve:

- Resources
- Cross-functional dependencies
- Customer issues
- Commercial performance
- Operational capacity
- Technology
- Talent
- Economics
- Policy
- Prioritization
- Decision rights

For every constraint, determine:

1. What exactly is preventing progress?
2. What evidence supports the diagnosis?
3. Can the accountable leader solve it?
4. If not, what leadership intervention is required?
5. What decision must be made?
6. Who owns execution after the decision?

> **Escalate the constraint, not the responsibility.**

---

## 05. Actions & Close

**5 minutes**

Do not end with a discussion summary.

End with commitments.

Capture:

| Decision / Action | Owner | Due Date | Expected Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
|  |  |  |  |

Before closing, confirm:

- Decisions made
- Actions agreed
- Owners
- Deadlines
- Escalations
- Items requiring follow-up

Everyone should know what happens next.

# Escalation Test

Not every problem belongs in the Weekly Operating Review.

Before escalating an issue, ask:

### Is the issue material?

Does it meaningfully affect a priority, KPI, customer, economics, people, risk, or strategic outcome?

### Can the accountable leader solve it?

If yes, it normally remains with that leader.

### Does it require cross-functional resolution?

If several functions must make a trade-off, escalation may be appropriate.

### Does it require authority the owner does not have?

If yes, leadership intervention may be required.

### Is a decision required now?

If not, determine whether the issue belongs in another management forum.

> **Senior leadership should remove organizational constraints, not absorb work that belongs elsewhere.**

# Meeting Rules

## 1. No surprises

Material issues should be surfaced early.

Bad news does not improve with age.

## 2. No presentations

Use pre-reads for information.

Use meeting time for decisions.

## 3. Evidence before opinion

Distinguish facts, assumptions, and interpretations.

## 4. Diagnose before solving

Do not prescribe an intervention before understanding the constraint.

## 5. One accountable owner

Committees can contribute.

Individuals own outcomes.

## 6. Decisions are recorded

Important decisions require rationale, ownership, expected outcome, and review.

## 7. Closed means closed

Completed actions leave the tracker.

Repeatedly missed commitments require intervention.

---

# What Should Not Be in This Meeting

The Weekly Operating Review should not become:

- A functional status update
- A presentation forum
- A detailed project meeting
- A brainstorming session
- A substitute for one-to-ones
- A place to solve every operational problem
- A meeting where every KPI receives equal time

The agenda exists to protect executive attention.

---

# Executive Output

At the end of every Weekly Operating Review, leadership should be able to state:

**What changed.**

**Where we are off plan.**

**Why.**

**What we decided.**

**What actions follow.**

**Who owns them.**

**When they are due.**

**What we will review next week.**

If those answers are unclear, the meeting has not finished its job.
